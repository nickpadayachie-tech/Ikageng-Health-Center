Ongoing care

What this service is

Minor ailments are common conditions that do not need a hospital but do need attention: coughs and colds, sore throats, ear infections, sinusitis, diarrhoea, constipation, mild skin conditions, burns, minor wounds, sprains and uncomplicated urinary tract infections.

A minor ailment should not be taken lightly, because what looks minor can become something more serious, particularly in the very young, the very old, or in someone with a long-term condition. We assess first, and we treat only what we are confident about.

A visit is also a good opportunity to check on other things: your blood pressure, your weight, your immunisation status, or how you are managing a long-term condition. Please ask.

Good to know

Time needed
Minor ailment appointments are generally short, though the clinic can be busy. Calling ahead helps us plan and shortens your wait.
Bring with you
A list of any medication you take, and the name of any medicine you have already started.
Also covered
We can check blood pressure, weight and chronic condition monitoring while you are here.

If your symptoms are severe, or if you are severely unwell, do not wait for an appointment. Please phone us on 067 575 2528 so that we can advise you straight away.

At your visit

What to expect

Every visit is a little different, but these are the steps you can expect for this service at Ikageng Health Centre.

Your symptoms

How long they have lasted, how severe they are and what you have already tried are discussed first.

Examination

The area concerned is examined properly, and where needed we listen to your chest or check your throat and ears.

A decision

We decide together whether this can be treated here, or whether it needs a referral to a hospital or a specialist.

Treatment

The right medicine and dose are given, with instructions you can follow at home.

Home care

Advice on fluids, rest, food and what you can do to feel better while you recover.

Warning signs

Clear instructions on which changes mean you should come back, and which mean you need care urgently.

Reasons to visit

Why people come to us

If any of these sound like you, please come in — or phone us first if you would like to talk it through before you do.

  • Cough, cold, sore throat, sinusitis or earache.
  • Diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation or stomach complaints.
  • Mild skin rashes, eczema, acne or minor infections.
  • Minor injuries: cuts, grazes, sprains, strains and small burns.
  • Urinary tract infection symptoms, or a repeat prescription for a condition that is stable.

Before you come

  • Bring your clinic card or any records you have.
  • Bring a list of any medication and supplements you take.
  • Bring the results of any tests done elsewhere.
  • Write down your questions beforehand — it helps.
  • Bring someone with you if you would like support.

All of our conversations are private. You are welcome to ask questions at any point, and you can ask to speak alone.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

If your question is not answered here, phone us on 067 575 2528 — we are happy to talk it through.

Do I need an appointment?

You can come in during our opening hours, but calling ahead on 067 575 2528 lets us set aside the right amount of time and avoid a long wait.

Can you give me a prescription for my usual medicine?

If your condition is stable and we have reviewed it, yes. If we need to see you first, we will do that and arrange the prescription at the same visit.

When should I come back?

We will tell you at the end of the visit. If symptoms are not improving after a few days, or if they get worse, please come back — and seek urgent care for the warning signs we mention.

Do you treat children’s minor ailments?

Yes. Children are assessed using the IMCI approach, which checks systematically for the signs that matter in a young child.
